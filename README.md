# Cholodenko's — Schematic Design

An eight-room bed and breakfast, drawn from the reference facade sketch: two stories plus an attic, 1920s storybook detailing, and a circulation plan that matches the brief exactly — check-in foyer flanked by two sitting parlors, corridors from each parlor to four guest rooms per side (two per floor), a dining room and kitchen behind the foyer, and laundry, storage, and the attic stair behind the second-floor landing.

## The idea

The reference facade reads grand: full columns, a wide awning, a name painted in gold across the front. An eight-room inn wants to feel like a good story rather than a courthouse, so the columns between the upper windows become slim, flat pilasters here — barely proud of the wall, closer to the seams of a dress than a colonnade. The bell-cast mansard stays. The arched windows and their iron Juliet rails stay. What goes quiet is anything that would make a guest feel small walking up to the door.

## Program, at a glance

- **8 guest rooms**, all en suite (4 ground floor, 4 second floor — 2 per side per floor)
- **2 sitting parlors** flanking the check-in foyer
- **Dining room + kitchen** behind the foyer, between the two guest wings
- **3 levels**: 2 stories plus an attic (dormer reading nook + unfinished storage/mechanical)
- **Redundant vertical circulation**: a stair at the end of each wing's corridor, plus a central main stair and a second-floor hall that connects both wings — no guest room depends on a single stair

## Jackson County, Oregon — code notes to raise early

These are flags for your architect and the county, not conclusions:

- **Occupancy classification** — eight guest rooms is past Oregon's small bed-and-breakfast allowance; expect an R-1 (transient) classification under the Oregon Structural Specialty Code, which brings in the commercial code rather than the residential one.
- **Egress** — R-1 occupancies generally need two ways out of every floor. This plan gives each guest room a path to its own wing stair and a second path across the central hall to the main stair.
- **Fire protection** — likely needs automatic sprinklers (NFPA 13 or 13R), a fire alarm system, one-hour-rated corridor walls, and self-closing doors between guest rooms and corridors.
- **Accessibility** — at least one guest room and an accessible route from parking to that room, the foyer, and the dining room. Room 101 is sited for this; final door hardware and route still need review.
- **Food service** — breakfast for overnight guests only is licensed differently than a full-service kitchen. Confirm with Jackson County Environmental Health / Oregon Health Authority before finalizing the kitchen.
- **Zoning (Ashland specifically)** — if the site is inside Ashland city limits, the Travelers' Accommodation ordinance limits unit counts in most residential zones and will likely require a Conditional Use Permit at eight rooms. Worth a planning department conversation before the drawings go further.

Codes are adopted on a cycle and can shift between when this is drawn and when it's built — treat every item above as a question for Jackson County Building Safety and a licensed Oregon architect, not an answer.
